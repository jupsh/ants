- **2026-10-18 ≈ 15:55 UTC — Resting-site literature read; notes in `docs/research/resting-sites.md`.** Read in full:
  the three Depickère 2004 papers, Amé et al. 2006, Jeanson et al. 2005. Boi et al. 1999: set-up, results and
  discussion. Heyman 2017, Mersch 2013 (the file is the supplement), Sendova-Franks & Franks 1995, Franks &
  Sendova-Franks 1992, Cole 1991: abstracts and passages found by search only. The J. Insect Behav. paper is a
  different set-up from Depickère 2004 Insect. Soc. (98-cm² ring vs 19.8-cm ring), so no overlap with what was read
  before. Nothing seen from any held-out source. **Proposals for the user, nothing adopted or run:**
  - **Option B's forager stop values were measured under red light** (Depickère 2004 Insect. Soc.; the red-light
    paper shows foragers aggregate poorly in red light, about like brood-tenders in total darkness: 80 % aggregated vs
    40 %). The Stroeymeyt nest is dark to the ants (IR-pass filter), so those forager rates are the wrong condition for
    it. This bears on `LASIUS_NEST_B` provenance, not on C1 / C2 (fitted rates).
  - **Site location and aggregation level are separate effects** (marking: where and how fast, not how many). A site
    model needs a persistent site field, neighbour-dependent resting and a capacity / spacing limit (Amé's S, a
    body-exclusion lattice as in Depickère's model). Q and T lacked the capacity term.
  - **Rest length depends on the neighbourhood** (Jeanson's short/long mixture vs N; Depickère's a·A^−b). Heavy-tailed
    observed rests may be a mixture over neighbourhoods, which links the rest-form question to packing.
  - **Synchrony may be spatial** (Boi: waves start at the brood pile at ≈ 0.01 cm/s; returning foragers disrupt the
    entrance group). Checkable from the tracks before any coupling mechanism.
  - These sharpen the data-side measurement proposed for the backlog (not yet filed; the user decides): stop rate vs
    still ants within a radius, still-run length vs neighbour count, departures after a passing mover, site-centroid
    stability, per-ant fidelity zones vs distance to the site and the queen, wall and centre separately.
